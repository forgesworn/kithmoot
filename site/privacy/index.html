Privacy notice

Draft published for review. Last updated 1 November 2026.

This page is a draft. It has not been reviewed by a lawyer and it is not final.

1. Who we are

KithMoot is open-source software published by ForgeSworn. This notice describes what our public website and update service handle, the public call relay and the Nostr relay we run, the independent services the apps contact, and the settings kept on your device. Since 1 November 2026 we again run a public call relay (TURN), which the apps use by default, and our Nostr relay, relay.trotters.cc, is again the first relay the apps use by default (sections 4.5 and 7). Private deployments arranged separately have their own operator and access arrangements (section 4.5). For UK data protection law we are the controller of the data our public website, our call relay, our Nostr relay and our contact route handle.

To ask about anything on this page, write to abuse@safety.forgesworn.dev.

Legal basis. What we handle is the address a web request or a call relay connection comes from, a call's timing and traffic volume while our relay carries it, what your device sends to our Nostr relay (encrypted events, the keys that sign them and when they arrive), and any correspondence you send us. Our basis for it is legitimate interests (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f)): we need it to run the app, this website, the update service, the call relay and the Nostr relay, and to answer you. We collect nothing else. You can object to this use by writing to the address above.

2. The short version

  • KithMoot encrypts room content on participants' devices. The public website and update service do not receive room messages, files or call media. Our call relay and our Nostr relay carry them encrypted and are given no key to open them (sections 4.5 and 7).
  • Since 1 November 2026 we run a public call relay (TURN) again. The web app, the desktop app from 0.1.124 and the Android app from 0.6.132 use it by default when a call cannot connect directly, unless you set your own relay. It sees your IP address and when and how much a call sends, not what is said, and keeps no logs of IP addresses (section 4.5).
  • relay.trotters.cc, a Nostr relay we run, is again the first relay the apps use by default. Anybody can read from it and write to it. What it keeps, and for how long, is in section 7.
  • In a call, the other people on it can see your device's local network addresses as well as its public one, unless you turn on Hide my IP address (section 4.5).
  • A member of a room can run a call relay for it. The desktop and Android apps can run one on the computer or phone, from desktop 0.1.120 and Android 0.6.130; it is off until you turn it on. KithMoot's makers run no part of it and are not told when it is used. People who use a member's relay see that member's internet address, and the member's relay sees theirs (section 4.5).
  • kithmoot.forgesworn.dev serves the app, this website and update information as static files, and gives out logins for our call relay. To start a call, the apps ask our call relay, or Google's and Cloudflare's public STUN servers, for your device's public address, unless you choose another server (section 4.3).
  • Until 22 October 2026 we also ran a file store, a message-drop service and meeting codes. Those stay closed. Our public call relay was closed from 22 October to 1 November 2026. The closed services' data and retained backups are described in section 4.4 and the closure record.
  • In the web app and the desktop app, files you send go to a public file server run by somebody else, unless you choose another server or none. They are encrypted on your device first (section 7).
  • Some defaults make your device contact other parties: relays, profile lookups, picture hosts and, for the desktop app, our site and GitHub (section 7). You can turn the profile lookups off.
  • We do not sell your data, show adverts, or run analytics or tracking.

3. Things we never collect

  • Your private key. Never sent to us in any KithMoot flow.
  • Room content at our servers. The website, the update service, our call relay and our Nostr relay do not hold room traffic keys. Participants' devices hold the keys needed to open their room's content; a TURN relay, ours included, is not given the WebRTC media session keys.
  • A membership list. There is no server-side list of the people in a room. Our Nostr relay, like any relay a room uses, holds the room's encrypted events signed by device keys, which lets it group those devices' activity (section 7). A member who runs a call relay keeps, on their own device, keys for the devices that use it (section 4.5); we never receive them.
  • Card or bank details. KithMoot has no payments functionality.

4. What our server sees, and why

4.1 The web server

Our web server serves the app, this website and the downloads. Like any web server it sees the address a request comes from, the time and the file asked for. ForgeSworn's host keeps no access logs for either KithMoot site, including for the automatic redirect from plain HTTP to HTTPS (as of 23 October 2026). Your address is therefore not written down there.

4.2 Update checks from the desktop app

Every desktop build except the Debian package checks our site for updates, 30 seconds after it starts and then on a timer: every ten minutes for the signed app for Macs with Apple silicon, and every hour for the Windows ZIP and the Linux tar.gz. When there is an update, the app downloads it from our site too. Each check is an ordinary request for a static file, so our web server sees the address it came from, the time, and the file it asked for, which says what kind of build is asking. There is no setting to turn the checks off. The access log is discarded (section 4.1). The Debian package does not check by itself: the system's package manager fetches its updates from GitHub (section 7).

4.3 STUN

STUN tells a device its own public address, so a call can try to connect directly. Since 1 November 2026 the web app, the desktop app from 0.1.124 and the Android app from 0.6.132 ask Google's public STUN server (stun.l.google.com) and then Cloudflare's (stun.cloudflare.com) whenever the device has no login from our call relay, as when a room first opens, and our call relay once it has one, unless you or the room chose another server. Desktop 0.1.102 to 0.1.123 and Android 0.6.106 to 0.6.131 ask Cloudflare's only. Older installed clients, including Android through 0.6.105 and desktop through 0.1.101, ask our server, which answers STUN again, on the call relay, since 1 November 2026. A web deploy does not update the desktop app's bundled copy, so installed versions may differ. Whichever STUN server is asked sees the device's IP address and port and when a call starts, and carries no call media. To choose your own STUN server, use Settings, Connections, “Your STUN server”; it replaces every other STUN address. Google and Cloudflare are in the United States (section 7).

4.4 Data left from closed services

The file store and meeting codes stopped on 22 October 2026, and on the same day we deleted what they held: 84 encrypted files, about 164 MB in all, each sealed on the sender's device, and the store of room links behind short codes. Two mirror copies of the files, on machines of our own that served them by hash over Tor, were deleted on 23 October 2026.

Copies can remain in the owner's encrypted, offline machine backups, which serve no downloads. They are retained by the owner's decision recorded in the closure record.

4.5 Call relay settings and private deployments

Our call relay. When two devices in a call cannot connect directly, the call goes through a TURN relay. Since 1 November 2026 we run a public one on kithmoot.forgesworn.dev. The web app, the desktop app from 0.1.124 and the Android app from 0.6.132 use it by default, for rooms that have not named their own call servers, unless you have set your own relay. Your device first asks our site for a login: no account is needed, the login lasts an hour, and the request carries no cookies. To stop one address taking too many, the login service counts requests by IP address in memory and forgets an address within about fifteen minutes of its last request; nothing is written down.

The relay sees your device's IP address and port, the addresses it passes your call's packets on to, when the call starts and ends, and how much it carries. The call's packets are encrypted (DTLS-SRTP) and the relay is not given the keys to the call, the room's name or its messages. It keeps no logs of IP addresses: its log output is discarded and its container keeps none, and our web server keeps no access logs (section 4.1). Each session is limited to 1 Mbit/s. Our hosting provider's own network records have not been audited, so we cannot promise that nobody else keeps one.

Your addresses in a call. Since 1 November 2026 your device tells the other people on a call its local network addresses (for example one beginning 192.168), as well as its public one, so that two devices on one network can connect directly. They travel inside the encrypted call setup; the other people on the call can see them. With Hide my IP address on, your call goes only through a relay, our relay included, so the other people on it see the relay's address and none of yours; the relay still sees yours.

A relay you set yourself. You can enter a relay address, username and password under Settings, Connections, Call relay (TURN); it replaces ours. These are settings for your device, not a public KithMoot relay account. The login is used with the relay addresses saved alongside it; it is not added to room invitations or published to Nostr. The web app keeps it in this browser profile's local storage. Android keeps it in a Keystore-encrypted file excluded from backups. Removing the relay deletes its saved login.

A relay you set receives your IP address, authentication identity, connection timing and traffic volume. It forwards encrypted call packets and is not given the WebRTC session keys. The relay's operator decides what metadata it keeps. Review that operator's privacy information and terms; this notice does not promise that an independently chosen provider keeps no logs.

A separate authenticated private relay is running for an arranged personal call. It is not offered through the public app's defaults or a public credential issuer. Its coturn and container logging are disabled; host and hosting-provider network logging have not been audited. Access and privacy arrangements for this private deployment are handled separately from the public software.

Public call relays. In the web app, and the Android app from version 0.6.113, you can turn on "Find public call relays on my Nostr relays" under Settings, Connections. It is off unless you turn it on. When it is on, the app asks the Nostr relays in your Connections list (on the shipped defaults, relay.trotters.cc, which we run, relay.primal.net, relay.ditto.pub and offchain.pub) for adverts that relay operators publish themselves. Each of those relays learns your device's IP address, the time and that a search for call relays was made. The search is made over a connection that is not signed in, so it does not tell them which Nostr key searched. KithMoot runs no list or directory of call relays and does not check, rank or vouch for anyone listed; a listed relay's region and notes are the operator's own claims.

When you check or use a relay you chose, its operator learns your IP address, the time and the call's traffic volume, and can link a call's renewed logins by IP address and timing, so it learns how long the call lasted. In the web app, the operator also learns which web app asked; Android sends no app name beyond its network library's default identifier. A web check makes two test connections, so the operator can recognise it as a check. An Android check asks for one relay address, with no camera or microphone use. The request carries no cookies or referrer. The operator issues an hour-long login. The app may reuse a check's still-valid login for your next call. Logins stay in memory on your device and are dropped when that call ends; the app never saves, publishes or adds them to a room, link or invitation. Other people on the call see that relay among your device's connection addresses, as with a relay you enter yourself. That operator, not us, decides what it keeps.

Call relays run by room members. A room member can run a call relay for the people in their rooms. The desktop app can run one on the computer it is installed on, and the Android app on the phone, from desktop 0.1.120 and Android 0.6.130. It is off unless you turn it on under Settings, Connections, "Call relay on this computer" (or "on this phone"). Each time you turn it on, the app first says what running it means, and you can stop it at any time.

On a computer it stops when the window closes, the computer sleeps or KithMoot quits. Once you have turned it on, it starts again by itself when the window reopens or the computer wakes, and, if you tick "Start it when KithMoot opens", when KithMoot next opens, until you press Stop. While it is on, "Relay on" shows above Settings. On a phone it runs in the background, with a notification showing where notifications are allowed for KithMoot, and only on Wi-Fi that is not metered, while the phone is charging and has at least half its battery.

The person who turns it on runs that relay. KithMoot's makers run no part of a member's relay and receive nothing from it: no counts, no check results and no reports. Its requests travel as encrypted messages over the Nostr relays in your Connections list, which on the defaults include ours, relay.trotters.cc, so our relay sees that requests were made and when, as the others on the list do, but not what they say. We do not hold the keys of a relay on your device; they stay on it, sealed by the operating system's keychain on a computer and by the Android Keystore on a phone.

Whoever runs a relay can see the internet address, the timing and the traffic volume of each person whose call goes through it, because their device connects to it, and the addresses that person's call packets are sent on to. To make sure only people admitted to the room can use it, the relay keeps, on the device it runs on, a key for each device that uses it in each room. The relay forwards encrypted packets and is not given the call's keys, room names or messages. People who use your relay see your internet address too, because the relay is on your connection. The built-in relay asks your router, using UPnP, NAT-PMP or PCP, to open what it needs and gives them back when it stops. A router that only offers permanent openings may keep them until KithMoot next starts if it crashes. The relay takes requests over the Nostr relays in your Connections list, as small encrypted messages, so those relays see that requests were made and when, as they do with other call setup. The built-in relay has a daily limit, 5 GB on a computer and 2 GB on a phone, and pauses until midnight when it is reached. KithMoot does not write down the addresses of the people who use a relay: the relay reports only counts to the app. The device's operating system, the router and the internet provider may keep their own records, and we cannot promise that nobody keeps one.

A member's relay serves the rooms that member made. In another room it is offered only if that member turns on "Share my call relay here" for it. If you have Hide my IP address on, your device uses a member's relay only after you choose to, naming that member, because they would see your address. The choice is kept on your device, for that member only, and is never published. Your own relay, and a relay you entered yourself, are not asked about. Turning Hide my IP on does not hide a host's own address from the people who use their relay, and the app says so before a host with Hide my IP on turns their relay on. So that a room can keep a relay while its owner is away, another member's device may pass the offer on, marked as a copy. With Hide my IP on, your device ignores a marked copy, because it cannot tell who runs that relay.

A member running a relay can ask another device in a room to check, from outside, that the relay can be reached. That device connects to the relay once, so the relay sees its internet address as on a call, and sends back only whether it worked. A device answers only the one member who offers that relay in the room, no more than once a minute, and not when Hide my IP is on and you have not chosen to use that member's relay. A check is not saved; the person who asked keeps its result in memory for as long as the relay runs.

4.6 What the app keeps on your device

The app stores what it needs to work in your browser (or, in the desktop app, in its profile on your computer; Android uses its own private storage). None of it is sent to us. (If you sign in with a signer that can encrypt, your saved-room bookmarks and read positions are also published to relays, encrypted to your own key, so your other devices can follow them.) It lives in the browser's local storage and IndexedDB, and in a service worker cache that lets the app load offline. It includes:

  • the rooms you have saved, with their links, which contain the room's first secret key, and the device keys, seal keys and credentials the app makes for each room;
  • for rooms this browser created, the secret that lets it manage the room;
  • the latest room keys the app has worked out, and an archive of room events you have received, still encrypted to the room, kept in IndexedDB under a key the browser will not export;
  • messages waiting to be sent, kept for up to 24 hours;
  • your display name, pinned rooms, contacts and blocked people, read positions, which of your messages others have confirmed (kept for seven days), and your settings: text size, send delay, delivery confirmations, call devices and effects, relay lists, the call relay login and STUN choice (section 4.5), and the privacy choices in section 7;
  • if you turn on public call relays (section 4.5), that choice, the newest advert seen from each operator, kept as the time and identifier of that advert so an older one cannot replace it and forgotten after two hours, and the last public relay that worked for you (its address and key). The adverts themselves and any login stay in memory only. Turning the setting off clears all of it;
  • in the desktop and Android apps, if you turn on the built-in call relay (section 4.5), the relay's own keys, sealed by the operating system's keychain or the Android Keystore; the relay's record of the device keys of the people who use it in each room; how much it relayed today; and, on a computer, whether to start it when KithMoot opens. On a phone these are kept in storage excluded from backups. The result of an outside check is held in memory only;
  • if you sign in with a Nostr signer, the sign-in details the signer library keeps so you stay signed in (your public key, the method and, for a remote signer, its connection link and a client key) under the signet:login. prefix.

Much of this is held as ordinary, unencrypted local storage, so anyone who can open your browser profile or computer can read it. We do not set advertising or tracking cookies and the app does not use storage to track you. Storage the app needs in order to work does not need your consent. Clearing the site's data in your browser, or choosing Forget this browser in the app, removes it, and with it your access to saved rooms on this device unless you have their links.

5. How long we keep information

The public web server keeps no access logs (section 4.1). Our call relay keeps no logs of IP addresses, and its login service forgets an address within about fifteen minutes (section 4.5). Our Nostr relay keeps events for the times given in section 7. Device-selected relay operators have their own retention arrangements (section 4.5). If you have written to us, that correspondence is kept for as long as we reasonably need it, and you can ask us to delete it.

6. Your rights

Because we hold so little that identifies anyone, most requests will come back "we do not hold that", which is itself the answer to a request. Where we do hold something identifiable to you, you can ask us to give you a copy, correct it, delete it, or restrict or stop using it. We will answer within one month.

Send requests to the address in section 1.

Content in a room. We cannot delete a message, a file or a call from a room or from anybody's device, and we cannot open what our relays carry. Our Nostr relay holds room events only in encrypted form, and deletes them on the schedule in section 7. To have something removed, leave the room, ask a keeper who runs it to remove you or close it, or, for your own signed public events, use the in-app tool that asks relays to delete them.

You can complain directly to us about our handling of personal data using the contact in section 1. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk).

7. Who else receives data

  • Public Nostr relays, including the independent relays the app suggests by default and any a room's creator adds, receive encrypted messages and connection metadata. The current roster format exposes device pseudonyms and a shared room or epoch tag, so an operator can group those devices' activity. Encrypted call-setup events still expose the recipient device tag, timing and sizes. Roster names and account credentials, and the call-setup contents, remain encrypted; public-profile lookups are described separately below. Encryption does not hide every relationship or activity pattern. Ephemeral events and expiration do not guarantee that an operator or observer deletes its copies.
  • Our Nostr relay, relay.trotters.cc. We run it. Since 1 November 2026 it is again the first relay the apps use by default for new installs and new rooms, followed by relay.primal.net, relay.ditto.pub and offchain.pub. Anybody can write to it and read from it, without signing in. It sees what any public relay sees (above): the encrypted events your device sends, the keys that sign them, the room tags, their times and sizes, and the IP address your device connects from. Its write policy records no IP addresses and its web front keeps no access logs. It deletes public posts, reposts, reactions and zap receipts (Nostr kinds 1, 6, 7 and 9735) after a day. For keys other than those held for the project's own work, it deletes private messages and media each time its clean-up runs, whatever their age; app data, reports and deletion requests after 90 days; and everything else after seven days. Copies other relays or readers took are outside our control.
  • Delivery confirmations go to the same relays as a room's chat. When your device receives a message in a room, it publishes a small encrypted confirmation so the sender can see that the message arrived. KithMoot does not send read receipts. Relays store each confirmation for up to seven days. A relay sees a one-off signing key, the room's existing encrypted chat tag, the time, the size and the network connection it arrived on; it cannot read which messages, which person or which device it is about. Because it arrives on the same connection your device uses for the room, a relay operator can still link it to your connection, and its timing shows that a device in the room was active. Members of the room can read confirmations and so can see when each member's device received each message. Leaving and deleting a room also asks relays to delete yours, though a relay may keep copies. Quiet chats do not send them, and you can turn them off under Settings, Messages.
  • Our hosting provider, which hosts the public app, website and update files, our call relay and its login service.

The app also makes your device contact the following. Each learns your device's IP address and the time, as any web request does.

  • Google's and Cloudflare's STUN servers, in a call. When the device has no login from our call relay, the web app, the desktop app from 0.1.124 and the Android app from 0.6.132 ask stun.l.google.com, run by Google LLC in the United States, and then stun.cloudflare.com, run by Cloudflare, Inc. in the United States, for your device's public address, as section 4.3 describes. Desktop 0.1.102 to 0.1.123 and Android 0.6.106 to 0.6.131 ask Cloudflare's only; kithmoot-agent asks the same defaults as the web app. Each learns your IP address and port and when the app makes the request. Neither carries messages, files or call media. To choose your own STUN server, use Settings, Connections, “Your STUN server”; it replaces both. A relay you set or a room's call-server settings can also decide which STUN server is contacted. Google and Cloudflare, not us, decide what they keep; their privacy policies are at policies.google.com/privacy and cloudflare.com/privacypolicy.
  • A call relay you set. An independently supplied or self-hosted TURN relay you enter on your device replaces ours and receives the connection metadata and encrypted packets described in section 4.5.
  • A call relay run by a room member. When two people in a call cannot connect directly, their devices may connect to a relay run by a member of the room, as section 4.5 describes. That member's relay sees your device's IP address, the time and how much it carries, and if you run the relay, the people who use it see yours. The member, not us, decides whether to keep that. We run no part of it and are not told when it is used.
  • Your Nostr relays and a public call relay you choose, if you turn on public call relays. The relays in your Connections list receive the search, and the operator you choose receives the check, the login request and the call's relayed packets, as section 4.5 describes. KithMoot runs no list of call relays and contacts no host of its own for this. Each operator decides what it keeps.
  • A public file server, when you send a file. In the web app, and the desktop app that loads it, files you send go to blossom.yakihonne.com, a public server run by Yakihonne LLC in the United States, unless you choose another server, or "Nowhere", under Settings, Privacy, "Where files go". The first time your device sends a file, the app names the server and says what it sees, and nothing goes to it until you press Send. Your file is encrypted and padded on your device first, so the server cannot open it. The server receives the encrypted file; its size, rounded up so it is not exact; the upload, signed by a key made for your device in that room, which lets the server tell that uploads in one room came from the same device; your IP address; and when files are uploaded and opened. Anyone who opens the file, the people in your room included, connects to the same server, which learns their IP addresses too. Yakihonne, not us, decides what it keeps and for how long; its terms are at yakihonne.com/terms. Your choice, and the address of the server it names, are saved on your device so the app does not ask again. If a later version changes the default server, the app asks again before sending anything to the new one. A copy of KithMoot built by somebody else may use its own default server instead, and the app names that server in the same way.
  • Signet, if you sign in with it. Choosing Signet opens or links you to mysignet.app, the website of the Signet signer service. It learns your device's IP address, the time, and a sign-in request that names KithMoot, a return address, a one-time challenge and, where relays carry the sign-in, the relays to use and a temporary public key made for that sign-in. Your signer then handles requests from the app to sign, and to encrypt saved-room data, over the relays named in the request (relay.primal.net is always included), so it sees those requests. Signet's own privacy information covers what it keeps; this notice does not. The web app does not contact mysignet.app unless you choose this sign-in.
  • GitHub, when you unlock a member sticker pack. If you are signed in and ask to unlock a member pack, your signer signs a short proof that stays on your device, and the app then downloads a public registry file from raw.githubusercontent.com. GitHub learns your device's IP address, the time and that the file was fetched. The request carries no cookies or referrer, and your public key is not sent. Nothing is requested unless you ask.
  • Relays asked for public profiles. To show people's chosen names and pictures, the app asks for the public Nostr profile of the people it shows you: those in a room, in your private chats list and in People, and, if you already know them, whoever sent you an invitation. It asks two public relays: purplepag.es (a relay that gathers profiles) and relay.primal.net. Releases before 1 November 2026 also asked relay.damus.io. For the people in the room you have open it also asks that room's own relays. Each learns the public keys it was asked about and the address the question came from. The room itself keeps those keys inside its encryption; this lookup is what hands them to relays in the clear, and a relay that also carries the room can link them to it. A key your browser made for itself when you joined with only a name is never asked about.
  • The domain in a Nostr address, if you turn checking on. If a person's profile names a Nostr address (name@domain) and you have ticked "Verify Nostr addresses", the app asks that domain to confirm it. The domain learns the name asked about and the address the question came from, and can infer that somebody is looking at that person. No cookies or referrer are sent.
  • The host of a profile picture. A picture in a person's profile is loaded from whatever host that person chose. The host learns the address it was fetched from and when, and so that somebody is looking at that person. No referrer is sent. The picture's owner may run that host.

In the web app, lookups and pictures for other people are off until you explicitly enable them; an existing saved choice is honoured. Older installed releases may still enable them by default. Use Settings, Privacy, "Show public profile pictures and names", and the "Show other people’s public profile pictures, names and Nostr addresses" switch to choose. The choice is saved on your device. Turning it off stops further directory lookups and removes other people’s loaded profiles and pictures; it cannot recall a request already sent. While Nostr account networking is enabled, the signed-in account’s own profile is read separately from the public profile relays, and its picture may be loaded from the account’s chosen host. Private invitations pauses these account lookups too. Checking Nostr addresses is off by default, under "Verify Nostr addresses" in the same place.

The desktop app also makes your device contact:

  • Our site, for updates, as section 4.2 describes.
  • GitHub, for the Debian package. If you install the Debian or Ubuntu package, your system's package manager fetches KithMoot's updates from the project's releases on GitHub, on your system's own update schedule. GitHub learns your device's address, the time, and that it is fetching KithMoot.
  • Google, in desktop versions before 0.1.91, on Windows and Linux. The spellchecker built into Electron, the framework the desktop app runs on, downloaded a dictionary for your language from a Google server, which learned your device's address, the time and the language asked for. Electron's documentation says nothing you type was sent. From 0.1.91 the app switches that download off, so it no longer contacts Google for spelling, and it has no spelling check on Windows and Linux. The app updates itself as section 4.2 describes, and the Debian package updates with your system. The Mac app uses the macOS spellchecker, which downloads nothing.

Every connection the desktop app makes, and a command to check each one, is listed on Check it yourself.

We do not use an email marketing service, an analytics company or an error-tracking company for this site.
