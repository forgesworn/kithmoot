    Room settings

    Turn on your camera or microphone:

    Hide what is behind you blur

    Blur is on the first time you turn the camera on. It is the computer's best guess at where you stop and your room starts, so edges, dim light and quick movement all let bits of the room through for a moment at a time. It uses battery, and on a slow machine it costs you smooth video. It makes your room harder to see. It is not a promise that nobody can see it.

    Change how your voice sounds off

    This disguises your voice. It does not make you anonymous. It moves your voice up or down and shifts its tone separately, which is enough that somebody who knows your voice will not place it straight away. It will not stop anybody who holds a recording of you and wants to match it, and it does not survive an expert comparison. Do not lean on it where being recognised would matter. It does not change your accent, your words or the way you put them, and anybody who knows those will still know you.

    Off: nothing that says it is an agent is sent your camera or microphone. It never leaves this device for them.

    What helping costs you

    Two people who cannot reach each other directly are one pair. This device will help three pairs at most, and stops being asked once it is full.

    The people you would be helping are already in the room with you. You can see and hear them now, and passing their video along gives you nothing you did not already have. What it does not let you do is pass one person off as another: every picture and voice is signed by whoever sent it, and a relabelled one is thrown out at the other end.

    Turn it off whenever you like, even in the middle of a call. Anybody you were helping finds another way round on their own, and neither your call nor theirs stops.
