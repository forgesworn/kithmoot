# KithMoot — brand identity

Generated by brand-forge, 24 August 2026.

## Name

**KithMoot** — a compound of two Old English words already in use across this portfolio.

- **kith** (OE *cȳþþu*, "knowledge, acquaintance") — those you know. In the shipped
  `kindred` primitive, kith is specifically a *mutual* verified bond: the other party
  verified you back, and there is a shared secret and Signet Words behind it.
- **moot** (OE *gemōt*, "assembly") — a meeting of the people to decide things.
  A `folkmoot` was the assembly of a district; the word survives in "town hall
  meeting", which is exactly the shape of the product.

Read together: **an assembly of those you know**. The name commits the product to
kindred-gated rooms, which is a design decision taken deliberately rather than a
happy accident of naming.

### Why not the alternatives

Twelve candidates were scanned. Every plain dictionary word was exhausted — `.com`
taken and GitHub taken for all of *skein*, *muster*, *parley*, *withy*, *sheaf*,
*kith*, *moot* and *coppice*. Two early favourites died on inspection:

| Candidate | Why it died |
|---|---|
| **Moot** (alone) | Every TLD checked was taken. Namespace crowded with direct category collisions — MootUp is a virtual events platform, plus a "moot" discussion app and an $18M-funded e-commerce company. |
| **Coppice** | Live Mac App Store app (coppiceapp.com), and `coppice` taken on GitHub, npm, crates.io and PyPI. |
| **MeshMoot** | Phonetic and visual collision with **Meshroom**, established open-source photogrammetry software. |
| **MootSwarm** | "Swarm" belongs to RelaySwarm; reusing it muddies which project is which. |
| **BraidRoom** | Scored equal on the rubric, but "braid" is a busy word in software (Braid the game, braid.software, Braid tracking software). |

### Scoring

62/80 against the nine-criterion weighted rubric — *Strong*, rising with the
kindred-gating commitment that resolves its one real weakness (the name promising
a social graph the product would otherwise not have).

Strongest on stress test (5/5 — coined compound, nothing found) and narrative
power (5/5 — inherits the kin/kith/ken triad). Weakest on global reach (2/5 —
"kith" is archaic even in English) and clarity (3/5 — needs a sentence of context
outside this portfolio).

## Tagline

> **A town hall nobody owns.**

Chosen because it carries the "citadel town hall meeting" feeling without the
in-joke, so it also lands on a stranger, a journalist or a funder, and states the
no-operator claim in five words.

Runners-up: *"The town hall your citadel is missing."* (right audience, dates the
project to a meme), *"Town hall, no town square."* (driest, needs a beat of
thought), *"Your kith, in one hall, on every device."* (only one carrying the
multi-device claim, but reads as a feature bullet).

## Tone

**Personality:** plain-spoken · precise about limits · unceremonious · warm about
people, cold about infrastructure.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| State what it does and what it cannot do | Sell |
| Use the reader's own words (room, hall, join, share) | Use exclamation marks or "Oops!" |
| Give numbers when a claim is measurable | Claim novelty to an expert audience |
| Name the failure mode before someone else finds it | Hide a limitation behind a hedge |

**Examples in context:**

| Situation | Copy |
|---|---|
| Greeting | Room's open. Send the link. |
| Success | Both devices are in. You're one person to everyone else. |
| Error | Couldn't reach two people directly. Routing through a forwarder — it can't read your media. |
| Nudge | Your laptop has the better uplink. Want it to carry the room? |
| Call to action | Start a room. |
